## The short answer

At Fenway Park, most daytime shade comes from roofs. For a day game, the Grandstand under the roof and the Pavilion clubs are the reliable shade. The bleachers, the Green Monster seats and the Field Box sit in the sun. Behind home plate and up the third-base line, shade arrives late in the game. For a night game, every seat is out of the sun by the middle innings.

Everything below comes from our shade calculator run on real 2025 Red Sox home games. To see every section inning by inning for your game, pick it on our [Fenway Park page](/stadium/redsox).

## A 1:35 PM game in July

These answers are for July 13, 2025 (1:35 PM vs the Rays), when the sun was high in the south-southwest at first pitch. The ones in the first two lists are rated High confidence unless we say otherwise: they hold even if the stands are a little taller or shorter than we measured.

### Shaded all game

- Grandstand sections 1–33 are under the roof and shaded through the 9th inning (Medium confidence for sections 1–6, whose front rows see some sun at the very end of a long game).
- The Pavilion clubs (the Dell Technologies Club and the Aura Club) are shaded for the whole game.

### In the sun all game

- Bleacher sections 34–43 are in the sun for the whole game.
- The Green Monster seats are in the sun for the whole game.
- On the first-base side, Field Box 9–38 is in the sun for the whole game.

Most Field Box seats are in the sun through the 4th inning; by the 9th, more than half of them have some shade.

Roof Box seats under the press box (R23–R37) are partly shaded through the 7th inning, and all but R33 through the 9th: it stands right behind them and reaches out over their last row, so their back rows are in its shade while the sun is behind them. R39–R43, past its end, are in the sun all game.

### Where shade arrives

The Loge Box behind home plate and up the third-base line, about sections 132–155, sits under the front of the Grandstand roof from about its 6th to 9th row: all of it but section 146, whose eight rows end just short of the roof, has some shade at first pitch, and every section is fully shaded by the 4th inning but 154 and 155, at the third-base end, which are by the 6th (Low confidence for most of them).

## A 4:10 PM game

Later starts put the sun further west, behind home plate and the third-base side. On July 12, 2025 (4:10 PM vs the Rays), Field Box 50–61, right behind home plate, are shaded from first pitch. On the first-base side, Field Box 9–38 is in the sun all game; most of Loge Box 87–106 is partly shaded at first pitch, under the roof's front edge, and in full sun by the 9th inning (Low confidence).

The Green Monster seats get no shade at all during 1:35 PM games in July, and during a 4:10 PM game only the front rows of M2 get any, from about the 9th inning, in the shadow of the rows behind them.

## Night games

On July 11, 2025 (7:10 PM vs the Rays), the sun was low in the west-northwest at first pitch and set during the game. Most Field Box seats are already in shade by the 2nd inning, and by the 6th inning every seat is out of the sun.

## How we worked this out

Our calculator places the sun with the NREL Solar Position Algorithm for the exact first pitch, then checks each row of each section against the stands and roofs around it, every half hour for three hours (a 2025 game averaged 2 hours 38 minutes). Fenway faces northeast: home plate to center field is 44°, measured from the foul lines on USGS aerial photos. Which rows sit under a roof comes from published seating guides; the heights of the stands are measured from USGS airborne lidar of the park (2021), which also confirms the Green Monster's 37 feet.

Every answer carries a confidence label. High means it stays the same if the park is turned a degree either way and the stands are somewhat taller or shorter than our measurements. Low means changes that small could flip it. Where we're not sure, we say so.

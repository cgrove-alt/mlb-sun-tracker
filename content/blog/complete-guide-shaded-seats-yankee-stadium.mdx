## The short answer

For a summer afternoon game at Yankee Stadium, shade comes from overhead: the deck above you or the roof over the upper deck. Seats in the open stay in the sun all game. In the upper deck the roof shades almost every section from first pitch; the far end of the third-base side gets its shade last. For a night game, the whole ballpark is out of the sun by the middle innings.

Everything below comes from our shade calculator run on real 2025 Yankees home games. To see every section inning by inning for your game, pick it on our [Yankee Stadium page](/stadium/yankees).

## A 1:05 PM game in July

These answers are for July 12, 2025 (1:05 PM vs the Cubs), when the sun was nearly due south and about 70° up at first pitch. Each one is rated High confidence unless we say otherwise: it holds even if the stands are a little taller or shorter than we measured.

### In the sun all game

- Bleacher sections 202–204 and 235–238 are in the sun for the whole game.
- Field level sections 103, 104, 135 and 136 are in the sun for the whole game.
- Terrace sections 305–307, 309 and 332–334 are in the sun through the 9th inning (Medium confidence for 333W, Low for 309). The exceptions are 309W, partly shaded until about the 6th and then shaded (Low confidence); 332B, in the sun until about the 9th; and 305 and 305W, under a bank of lights on the roof: 305 is partly shaded until about the 6th, then in the sun, and 305W through the 9th (Medium confidence for these three).
- The Legends seats (sections 011–029) are in the sun through the 9th inning (Medium confidence for most of them).

### Partly shaded

- Main level sections 232 and 233 are partly shaded until about the 7th inning: the back rows sit under the Terrace deck above, the front rows get sun. Then the sun moves behind the stand and they are shaded (Medium confidence). 234W is partly shaded until about the 9th, then shaded; 234 through the 9th, then shaded (Medium confidence for both).
- The rest of 205–213 and 228–231 are never in full sun, and 211–213 are shaded all game (Low confidence for most of these).
- Most field level sections between 105 and 129 are partly shaded through the 7th inning, with the front rows in the sun. By the 9th, about three in ten are fully shaded.
- Terrace section 308 is partly shaded until about the 7th inning: a bank of lights on the roof above shades its back rows.

If you want shade at field or main level, ask for a row near the back of the section.

### Where shade arrives

The roof over the Grandstand (400s) gives the most shade in the ballpark: it reaches over all but each section's front row. Most 400-level sections (30 of 36) are shaded at first pitch, and all of them by the 7th inning.

Sections 405–430 are shaded from first pitch through at least the 7th inning, and the third-base end, 432–434, gets its shade between the 2nd and the 7th.

## Night games

On July 10, 2025 (7:05 PM vs the Mariners), the sun was low in the west-northwest at first pitch and set during the game. By the 2nd inning, more than nine in ten sections in the 100s and 200s are in shade, and by the 6th inning the whole ballpark is.

## April and September

The sun is lower in spring and early fall, so shade arrives earlier. On April 13, 2025 (1:35 PM vs the Giants), about a quarter of the terrace sections were shaded at first pitch and five in six by the end of the game; on July 12 none were shaded before the 4th inning, and only two (313 and 314, under banks of lights on the roof) before the 6th (Low confidence).

## How we worked this out

Our calculator places the sun with the NREL Solar Position Algorithm for the exact first pitch, then checks each row of each section against the decks and roof around it, every half hour for three hours (a 2025 game averaged 2 hours 38 minutes). Yankee Stadium faces east-northeast: home plate to center field is 75°, measured from USGS aerial photos. The heights of the stands and the roof canopy are measured from USGS airborne lidar of the ballpark (2014); the canopy's height matches New York City's building records.

Every answer carries a confidence label. High means it stays the same if the stadium is turned a degree either way and the decks are somewhat taller or shorter than our measurements. Low means changes that small could flip it. Where we're not sure, we say so.
